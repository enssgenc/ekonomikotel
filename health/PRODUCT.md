# Cappadocia Health

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Initial implementation: static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, built with Vite. The user did not specify a framework or deployment target.

## Users

People considering a health treatment while visiting Cappadocia, who need to understand possible treatment areas and how medical care and travel can be coordinated safely.

## Product Purpose

Present the concept of a Cappadocia health journey: treatment decisions belong to authorized health institutions and physicians; travel, accommodation, and optional visits are planned around the individual care and recovery plan.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Turkish language first, with full English, German, Russian, Arabic, and French views. Arabic uses right-to-left layout.
- A broad, searchable treatment catalog with plain-language explanations.
- Cappadocia travel information and a clear account of how the two parts of the journey fit together.
- Six editorial blog articles with localized copy, generated photography, primary sources, and related reading.
- No treatment result, recovery date, flight, balloon ride, or excursion is guaranteed.
- Real hospital names, physician names, contact details, authorization documents, prices, and commercial partnerships have not yet been supplied. Do not invent them or publish claims that depend on them.
- Site is a local design and content prototype. No inquiry backend or public deployment is included yet.

## Brand Commitments

“Cappadocia Health” is a provisional name approved by the user as a working example. The site should feel distinctive, grounded in Cappadocia, and avoid generic generated-site styling.

The user rejected the first dark, flat visual design. The replacement should stay light, layered, and visually richer, with health services and trust as the dominant first-screen message; Cappadocia supports that message.

## Evidence on Hand

User-provided concept and brand direction; official Turkish health tourism and destination information recorded in CONTENT_SOURCES.md. Generated visuals are editorial illustrations, not photographs of partner institutions or actual patients.

## Product Principles

1. Put the medical decision before the itinerary.
2. Make the range of options easy to scan without presenting general information as personal advice.
3. Let the destination feel specific and inviting without promising activities during recovery.
4. Keep institution and partnership claims tied to verifiable facts.
